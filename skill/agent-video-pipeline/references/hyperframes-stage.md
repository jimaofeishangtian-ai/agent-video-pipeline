# HyperFrames stage

HyperFrames owns creative direction, visual grammar, layered prototypes, and machine-readable design tokens. For recorded talking-head footage, it follows the ChatCut A-roll lock so the storyboard uses real timing.

## Inputs and outputs

Inputs: `timeline-manifest.json`, `edit-lock.json`, brand/reference assets, and platform format.

Outputs: `BRIEF.md`, `frame.md`, `STORYBOARD.md`, `creative-contract.json`, and a seekable layered prototype.

## Build method

1. Map the locked transcript to hook, promise, evidence, mechanism, payoff/CTA without changing approved speech.
2. When no direction exists, propose up to three genuinely different directions. State mood, cultural reference, palette/type logic, depth, motion character, and when each should not be used.
3. Lock one direction before scaling all beats.
4. For every beat define viewer job, visual metaphor/world, foreground/midground/background, focal hierarchy, motion verb, transition, SFX cue, actual frame range, caption zone, and subject-protected zones.
5. Reserve the real caption band and speaker region before placing cards or demo panels.
6. Avoid generic AI defaults unless conceptually justified: purple haze, gradient text, equal cards, all-centered composition, and web-page screenshot layouts.
7. Make the prototype deterministic and seekable. Review representative hook, dense middle, Seedance boundary, and CTA frames in its live timeline; do not render a review video.
8. Write `creative-contract.json` with stable IDs and exact tokens. Remotion consumes data and layers, not screenshots.

## Shot routing

- Photographic motion, spatial emotion, physical action, and lens-driven transitions may route to Seedance.
- Typography, UI, diagrams, charts, and abstract relationships stay in HyperFrames/Remotion.
- A simulated “cinematic demo” must be marked `simulated`; it is not evidence of a Seedance call.
